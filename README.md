# Sowlith — Global Medical University Research

> **Purpose:** Identify the safest, academically strongest, clinically rigorous and professionally viable **MBBS or equivalent first medical-degree programme outside India** for an Indian student, while keeping **total university fees for the entire programme strictly below ₹60,00,000**.
>
> **Status (16 November 2026):** Research framework established. **No university is yet verified as meeting all requirements.** Any university mentioned in exploratory research is a *candidate*, never an approved recommendation without evidence.

## The family's five priorities — in this exact order

| Priority | What matters | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Total university fees** | Complete medical degree, not annual tuition | **Hard maximum: below ₹60 lakh** for the entire course. Aim for **₹50 lakh or less** to leave financial headroom. |
| **2. Full medical knowledge + live practical training** | Rigorous theory, anatomy and laboratory teaching, early clinical contact, actual supervised patient care, procedural skills, rotations, simulation and meaningful internship | Verify actual curriculum, teaching hospitals and clinical experience. Rankings and marketing claims are insufficient. |
| **3. NMC regulatory pathway** | Ability of an Indian graduate to qualify for registration and medical practice in India | Establish compliance with **applicable National Medical Commission (NMC) rules**; never mislabel a foreign university “NMC-certified.” |
| **4. Safety** | Campus, hostels, city and clinical-placement travel; low risk of violence, ragging, harassment and discrimination | Seek independent incident data and enforceable protection measures. **No university is guaranteed crime-free.** |
| **5. Career opportunities** | Quality of internships, licensing, postgraduate/residency pathways, employability, research and international mobility | Verify actual eligibility and outcomes for **international students**, not just domestic graduates. |

These priorities are **not** an invitation to trade away a hard requirement. An over-budget course, demonstrably inadequate clinical training or a non-compliant registration pathway **cannot** become a recommendation because it scores highly elsewhere.

## Scope and fixed constraints

- **Study destinations:** Research **globally, excluding India as the university destination**. India remains important for the student's possible future medical registration.
- **Qualification:** First professional undergraduate medical degree (MBBS, MD-equivalent or other qualifying basic medical degree), **not** nursing, dentistry, pharmacy or postgraduate medicine.
- **Budget:** The user means **university fees only**, across **all years of the entire medical programme**. Track tuition **plus compulsory university charges** (registration, lab, clinical teaching, examination, mandatory institutional charges, and mandatory university internship fees if any). Keep accommodation, meals, travel, visa, insurance and personal living costs in a **separate** estimate, never mixed into the university-fee ceiling.
- **Strict threshold:** The complete projected university-fee total must be **less than ₹60,00,000**. A figure equal to or above the ceiling fails. Prefer comfortably under ₹50,00,000 when quality and compliance are comparable.
- **Financial caution:** Convert each fee using a **dated and sourced FX rate** and model later-year tuition changes, currency movements and any extra institutional years. Do **not** assume the first-year fee is fixed for the entire degree. A current-price estimate below the ceiling with unknown escalation is **PROVISIONAL**, not a verified budget pass.
- **Language:** Establish the actual **instruction and clinical teaching language**, plus any host-country language requirement for speaking with patients.
- **Admission:** Record entrance examination, school marks, English-test, application deadlines and international seat rules; Indian eligibility and NEET status are **not yet supplied**. Do not fabricate them.
- **Ethics:** No fabricated approvals, rates, crime-free guarantees, admission promises, internship guarantees, employment guarantees or personal student details.

## Critical correction: “NMC-certified foreign university”

**NMC does not endorse a list of foreign medical institutions/universities for MBBS or equivalent courses.** The correct task is **programme-specific compliance and licensing eligibility**, not collecting “NMC-approved abroad” advertisements.

Primary official reference: [NMC — For Students to Study Abroad](https://www.nmc.org.in/information-desk/for-students-to-study-in-abroad/1000/).

As an initial checklist for new entrants, verify the applicable **Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations, 2021**, subsequent notices, FAQs and rules in force for the relevant admission/graduation cohort. The original regulations specify, among other conditions:

1. **At least 54 months** of qualifying foreign medical study.
2. **At least 12 months of internship at the same foreign medical institution**.
3. **English-medium degree instruction** and required medical subjects.
4. Qualification for **registration/licensure in the degree-awarding country**, on terms comparable to its citizens.
5. Applicable **supervised internship in India** and **Indian licensing examination/registration** requirements.

Check for restrictions on split-campus/offshore teaching, third-country clinical rotations, online components, gaps, transfers and the real structure of the internship under **the latest NMC interpretation**. A sixth-year “clinical practical”, 36-week placement or traineeship is **not automatically** equivalent to a separate 12-month internship. Obtain **written university and home-country regulator documentation**, and seek NMC clarification for ambiguous cases. Do **not** promise that an email from a university binds NMC.

Official sources:
- [NMC FMGL Regulations, 2021 — Gazette PDF](https://www.nmc.org.in/MCIRest/open/getDocument?path=%2FDocuments%2FPublic%2FPortal%2FLatestNews%2F231275.pdf)
- [NMC Rules and Regulations — including 2026 FAQs](https://nmc.org.in/page/rules-regulations-rules-regulations-nmc)
- [NMC — Equivalence of Foreign Medical Qualification](https://nmc.org.in/page/equivalence-of-foreign-medical-qualification)
- [NMC 1 April 2026 foreign-study alert](https://www.nmc.org.in/MCIRest/open/getDocument?path=%2FDocuments%2FPublic%2FPortal%2FLatestNews%2FAdveisry_merged_admninforeignUniv.pdf)
- [World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS)](https://search.wdoms.org/) — **a listing is not accreditation, NMC endorsement or proof of India licensing eligibility**.

**As rules and exam implementations may change, re-check official NMC/NBEMS notices at the moment of decision.** Never infer approval from WDOMS, WHO references, an agency's WFME recognition, an agent's blog or an admissions consultant's statement.

## Research and evidence requirements

### A. Entire-course fees — priority 1

For each candidate record: academic years, separately required internship length, fee for **each year** in original currency, mandatory institutional fees, quoted fixed-fee guarantees, scholarship conditions, applicable international-student fee category and potential extra placement years. Compute:

**Projected university fees (INR) = sum of all annual tuition and mandatory university fees converted at documented FX assumptions.**

Report **current-fee baseline** and a **conservative escalation/FX scenario**. Show every assumption. If full-program cost cannot be established or could breach ₹60 lakh, mark **UNKNOWN / AT RISK** and do not certify it as a budget-qualified final recommendation.

### B. Knowledge and live clinical training — priority 2

Collect the actual prospectus, year-by-year syllabus, dissection/cadaver teaching, pathology/labs, simulation and OSCE/skills training; teaching-hospital names and relationships; student access to real patients; clinical rotations by specialty; contact hours; supervised procedures; assessed patient encounters; class size and tutor ratio (where published); patient volume (if verifiable); internship location, duration and responsibilities; feedback from current students; and accreditation/inspection findings. Distinguish **observing**, **simulation** and **supervised hands-on patient care**. Do not infer that all students get procedures simply because a hospital has many beds.

### C. NMC and destination-country legal pathway — priority 3

For each specific programme, build a requirement-by-requirement compliance matrix with primary documentary evidence. Verify degree-awarding institution, recognition by its domestic education/medical regulator, study duration, complete subject list, medium of instruction, all teaching locations, internship **dates and institution**, domestic registration rights **for foreign citizens/graduates**, examinations and country-specific conditions. Record **PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED** for each requirement. If any essential requirement fails, **do not recommend for an India-practice pathway**.

### D. Personal and campus safety — priority 4

Investigate national and city crime statistics **separately from** university/hostel data. Seek 3+ years of available police and campus safety reports, recent serious incidents, anti-ragging/anti-hazing policy and enforcement, harassment reporting, independent complaints support, dormitory access/security, emergency response, late hospital-shift transport, neighbourhood safety and international-student testimony. Verify sources and note non-public data. **“No incidents found” is not “zero crime.”** No 100%-crime-free claims.

### E. Career opportunity — priority 5

Distinguish national citizens' privileges from international student eligibility. Verify whether a foreign graduate can legally undertake **the internship**, gain **full local licensure**, compete for residency/specialty training, work in hospitals, obtain a visa and pursue research. Use published graduate outcomes if available; distinguish anecdote from measured placement rates. Compare India-return licensing and other country pathways separately.

## Decision method: evidence gates before ranking

Each candidate receives five statuses:

- **BUDGET:** PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED / AT RISK
- **CLINICAL EDUCATION:** PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED
- **INDIA LICENSING PATHWAY:** PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED (legal final authority belongs to regulators)
- **SAFETY:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED / MATERIAL CONCERNS / NOT VERIFIED — **never “crime-free certified”**
- **CAREER:** DOCUMENTED / LIMITED / NOT VERIFIED

**Final recommended shortlist:** only universities with a defensible **budget PASS**, strong documented clinical teaching, and no identified NMC disqualifier **after all essential legal criteria have been verified**. Independently assessed safety and career outcomes then distinguish eligible options. If no candidate clears the gates, report **no verified recommendation** rather than invent a winner.

All comparative scores, if used, must disclose their components and source reliability. **Never let a weighted average conceal a failed hard gate.**

## Research workflow for humans and AI agents

1. **Discover worldwide** — survey relevant countries across Europe, Asia, Central Asia, the Americas, Africa and Oceania; do not assume any country is safe, affordable or compliant.
2. **Pre-screen fees and eligibility** — remove definite over-budget programmes; document excluded candidates with sources so work is not repeated.
3. **Verify live clinical training** — inspect curriculum and placements, not only school rankings.
4. **Audit NMC and local licensure** — collect university/regulator evidence and record unresolved questions.
5. **Audit safety independently** — city, campus, hostel, clinics and student experience.
6. **Audit career paths** — internship, residency, job, research and India return.
7. **Peer-review and produce a shortlist** — include contrary evidence, disqualifiers, uncertainty, dated FX and audit trail.

**Source priority:** official Indian regulator and host-country regulator → official university programme/fee/clinical documents → official hospital and publicly accountable safety records → independent accreditation and credible research → corroborated student experience. Commercial admission-agent pages are lead-generation only, **never sole proof**.

Every substantive claim needs **source URL, publisher, publication/effective year, date accessed, exact programme/campus and short supporting quotation or pinpoint**. Keep conflicting evidence visible and seek written clarification; do not silently resolve it in favour of a candidate.

## Current research state and important caveats

**The repository has not yet established an eligible, safe, cost-compliant winner.** Previous conversational leads (for example Bulgaria, Serbia, Romania and Italy) are **leads for investigation only**. Earlier single-year tuition estimates must be independently rechecked; a low advertised tuition or an internship-sounding programme title is not enough.

**Unknown student details** include academic status/marks, NEET qualification, intended admission year, language capability and desired long-term practice country. Research can proceed without inventing these details; admission readiness remains **PENDING STUDENT DETAILS**.
